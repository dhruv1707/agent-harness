# Brief Samples

Real scripts sent to creators, verbatim. **Do not clean these up.** The line breaks, the
trailing thoughts, the sentence fragments and the ellipses are the signal — that is how the
brand sounds out loud. Match this register over anything inferred from general marketing
writing.

Note the form: these are not bulleted briefs. They are full first-person scripts, written
to be read aloud, broken one beat per line, ending in `CTA`.

## Notes on the register

Observed across all three samples — these are descriptions of what is actually there, not
rules invented on top of them.

**The quoted lines below are illustrations of a pattern, not approved phrasing to reuse.**
Only the product boilerplate in `brand-voice.md` is copied word for word. A new script
should hit the same beats with its own images.

- **First person, one narrator, past tense.** The creator tells what happened to her.
- **One beat per line.** Line breaks do the pacing work. Sentences are short and often
  fragments. Read them aloud and the breaks are where you'd breathe.
- **Opens mid-thought, on a specific private moment** — not on the product, not on a
  compliment, not on a premise the viewer has to accept first.
- **The turn is a realization**, and it is always physical and concrete: the pillow was
  *warm*, the body was *running hotter than it used to*.
- **The product explanation is near-identical boilerplate** in every script (see
  `brand-voice.md`). It is not rewritten each time — it is dropped in.
- **A soft disclaimer near the end** deflates the claim before it overreaches:
  "I'm not saying a pillow fixes your relationship."
- **Casual asides carry the voice**: "It was a whole thing.", "Seriously.", "game changer.",
  "just get two."
- Ellipses (`…`) mark a trailing thought before a punchline. Em dashes are rare.
- Ends on the offer or the instruction, then `CTA`. No summary, no recap.
