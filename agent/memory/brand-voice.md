# Brand Voice — Hugl

Everything marked **confirmed** is taken verbatim from the approved scripts in
`brief-samples.md`. Everything marked _(unconfirmed)_ is a gap — do not fill it by
inference, ask a human.

## The product

**Confirmed from approved scripts:**

- Hugl is a **U-shaped cooling body pillow**.
- It **wraps around you** and supports your **neck, back, hips, and knees at the same time**.
- The **fabric helps pull heat away from your body** instead of trapping warmth against you.
- It **feels cool the second you get into it**, and does not "just feel cold for five minutes
  and then turn into a regular pillow."
- It replaces the pile — the several separate pillows people wedge between knees, behind the
  back, under an arm.
- Sold individually and as a **two-pack**, positioned for couples who both run hot.
- Current offer as written in an approved script: **free cooling throw and eye mask**.

_(unconfirmed — ask before a creator says any of these on camera)_: price, materials and
fill, cover washability, shipping and returns, warranty, size and weight, cooling
technology by name, any temperature or degree figure.

## Who it talks to

Confirmed from the scripts: women who are waking up hot at night, who share a bed with a
partner who also runs hot, and who are in or near **perimenopause**. The scripts speak to
her directly and about her own body — not to a general "sleep better" audience.

## How the brand talks

- **First person, past tense, one narrator.** She tells what happened to her. Never
  "customers report" or "studies show."
- **One beat per line.** Short sentences and fragments. Line breaks set the pace.
- **Concrete and physical.** "Hair sticking to my neck." "The pillow was warm." Never
  abstract benefit language.
- **Casual, spoken, a little self-deprecating.** "It was a whole thing." "Seriously."
- **Deflate before overreaching.** The scripts undercut their own claim on purpose:
  "I'm not saying a pillow fixes your relationship."

## Approved phrasing — usable verbatim

Drop these in rather than rewriting them. This wording recurs near-identically in every
approved script, which is what makes it safe:

> Hugl is a U-shaped cooling body pillow, so it wraps around you and supports your neck,
> back, hips, and knees at the same time.

> And the fabric helps pull heat away from your body instead of trapping warmth against you
> all night.

> If you and your partner both run hot at night, just get the two-pack.

## Claims — never without sign-off

- **Any health or medical claim**, including anything framed as treating, relieving or
  improving perimenopause, menopause, hot flashes, night sweats, insomnia, or pain.
  Perimenopause appears in an approved script only as the narrator's own lived context
  ("part of it was definitely perimenopause"), never as something the product addresses.
  Keep that distinction exactly.
- **Any number**: degrees of cooling, percentage, hours of sleep, customer counts, ratings.
- **Comparatives against a named competitor.** The scripts compare only to "my old body
  pillow" and to a generic pile of pillows.
- **Relationship or intimacy outcomes.** The scripts deliberately stop short — mirror that
  hedge, do not drop it.

## Never say

- Agency filler: leverage, utilize, elevate, resonate, game-changing _(as a written
  descriptor — "game changer" as the narrator's spoken aside is in an approved script)_,
  unlock, in today's fast-paced world.
- Clinical register: "clinically proven", "thermoregulation", "sleep architecture."
- Second-person prescription in the script body. The scripts persuade by telling a story in
  first person, not by instructing the viewer.
